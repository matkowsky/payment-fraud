**Changed, under "S9.4 Memory, dependence and revision", by [#44](https://github.com/matkowsky/payment-fraud/pull/44):**

Source ancestry is necessary but not sufficient to establish independent support. A formal treatment of evidence dependence distinguishes independent observations from reports derived from shared evidence <ins>and requires a dependence model as well as ancestry </ins>\[71\].<ins> It shows that no rule that sees only report content can, in general, tell replication from independent corroboration. In its experiments, with one small language model reading synthetic documents, repeated extractions from one document had correlated errors \(estimated correlation 0.72, in an exploratory analysis\), and a deduplication method that clustered reports by text similarity tracked how they were worded far more than their ancestry.</ins> C3 follows the same concern: several agents repeating one report do not create several independent observations.<ins> Records therefore carry their roots, their producer and links to the agent outputs that informed them, and a record without a producer, or without inputs unless it is a source observation, adds no root \(manuscript Section 6.4\). The specification does not estimate how strong the dependence is; quality review judges that from these records.</ins> A released conclusion can guide the next query, but evidence for that conclusion must remain traceable to the underlying records.
