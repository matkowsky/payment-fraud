**Changed, under "2.1 Decisions shape the outcomes used to judge them", by [#26](https://github.com/matkowsky/payment-fraud/pull/26):**

When a decision determines which cases receive outcome labels, evaluation faces the selective labels problem \[1, 2\]. Counterfactual analysis of logged decisions is most direct when the decisions were randomized with known probabilities. Each logged sample is then reweighted to reflect its probability under another decision rule, so that average outcomes under that rule can be estimated \[3\]. All of these methods require assumptions about which comparisons the data support \[4\].<del> Consumer credit met the same problem earlier as reject inference: a lender observes repayment only for the applicants it financed and asks whether, and under what assumptions, the outcomes of rejected applicants can be inferred. A review of common reject-inference methods finds their assumptions mostly implicit. C3 makes a narrower, stronger choice for organizational memory: a person releases every conclusion, and the released conclusion remains context or hypothesis.
